Mission Loop

How the avoidance flow works

1. Detect

Live collision checks and near-future convergence warnings flag at-risk satellites.

2. Project

Satellite and debris motion are projected forward into the planner grid across future ticks.

3. Search

A* explores lane/time moves to find a safe corridor through the forecast hazard field.

4. Execute

The selected satellite follows the computed avoidance route instead of remaining on its original path.

System Notes

How the A* model maps to orbit

The planner is intended as a time-expanded orbital model. Each route step pushes the mission one tick forward while allowing a limited lane shift, so the search remains interpretable and tied to the live motion of satellites and debris in the scene.

Known Limits

What remains abstracted

No real orbital burns

Lane changes are stylized avoidance maneuvers, not full physics-based transfer calculations.

No probabilistic uncertainty

Tracked objects are treated as known exactly, without sensor noise or confidence envelopes.

Single active reroute

One avoidance plan runs at a time for clarity and to preserve performance in a single-file build.

Educational simplification

The model is explainable and visual first, even though the planning is now tied to live simulation state.
